Our story

Two kitchens, one drafty barn, and a lot of wax

Ember & Oak started in 2015 when Maya Okafor melted down the stubs of every candle in her apartment because she couldn't afford new ones — and decided the blend she made smelled better than anything at the store. Her brother Sam built the first pouring rig out of a baking rack and a stockpot.

Four years later we pour about six hundred candles a week in a converted tannery on the Fishkill Creek, still in batches small enough that every single jar is wicked, poured, and labeled by a person whose name you could learn.

The workshop

Where the pouring happens

Our workshop keeps the wax at exactly 135°F before every pour — hot enough to bind fragrance, cool enough to keep it from burning off. Each batch is forty jars, never more, and every one cures for two full weeks before it goes on a shelf.

We buy soy wax grown and processed within the U.S., cotton-core wicks from a family mill in Rhode Island, and our glass comes back to us through a jar-return program: bring an empty, get $4 off your next candle.

What we won't compromise on

Clean ingredients

100% soy wax, phthalate-free fragrance, zinc-free wicks. Nothing in the jar we wouldn't burn in a nursery.

Small batches

Forty jars at a time means we catch every air bubble, crooked wick, and weak pour before you ever see it.

Honest pricing

A $28 candle should burn for 50 hours, not 20. We publish burn times for every product and stand behind them.
