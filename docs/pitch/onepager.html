⿻ StepFree

The accessibility engineer that fixes your code. And proves it.

An agentic workflow that repairs WCAG violations in a website's source, verifies every fix with deterministic re-scans, content-integrity and visual checks, and routes judgment calls to a human — with evidence a business owner can hand to their lawyer.

How it works — agents with a deterministic spine

scan (axe-core) → route to five specialist agents (structure · images¹ · forms · ARIA · contrast²) → verify every fix: re-scan gate + word-level content integrity + pixel diff localized to the elements each fix may touch → retry or roll back what fails → memory keeps color and alt-text conventions consistent across pages → beyond-scanner reviewer hunts what axe can't see (images of text, fake div-buttons, keyboard traps) → human review queue where every judgment call arrives with a drafted fix → evidence report.

¹ the image agent looks at every image (vision) before writing alt text — no filename guessing.  ² contrast fixes come from a math tool that finds the closest compliant color preserving the brand hue — models supply judgment, never arithmetic. Design choices map 1:1 to documented failure modes of unverified LLM fixes (24 improvements vs 20 regressions, arXiv:2608.24913; ~30% structural damage, arXiv:2605.27716).

Business

  • Wedge: $149 one-time "fix & prove" run per site; $49/mo continuous monitor-and-fix. Agency tier $499/mo for 20 client sites — agencies hold thousands of 2019-era SMB sites and eat the legal risk conversation.
  • Unit economics: ~$2 measured compute per site run → 95%+ gross margin at the wedge price.
  • Market: digital-accessibility software ~$1B by 2030, but the spend it replaces — audits, remediation projects, legal exposure across 5,000+ suits/yr — is far larger. Incumbent revenue (AudioEye $40M, accessiBe ~$50M) proves willingness to pay for a product that doesn't actually fix code.

Why we win

  • Honesty as strategy: automation covers ~57% of issues (Deque). We claim verified fixes for the detectable layer and route the rest to humans — the exact claim discipline the FTC fined the market leader for lacking.
  • Verification is the moat: anyone can prompt an LLM to "fix accessibility". Shipping it to a stranger's production site takes the guardrail stack — and the evidence trail that survives a legal letter.
  • The curb-cut effect: curb cuts were built for wheelchairs and ended up serving everyone. Same here: clearer labels, readable contrast, working keyboards — a better site for every visitor.
